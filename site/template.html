What does a language model think water does?

We asked 28 language models about pure water at thousands of temperatures and pressures, drew the phase diagram each one implies, and graded it against IAPWS. Answering instantly, none beats a sketch made from four remembered facts. Allowed to reason, half of the 18 models tested both ways beat it, and the best are almost exact.

Across generations the answer depends on the curve. Instantly, OpenAI's boiling curve moved toward the truth and the top of its melting line swung past it, but neither move is resolved over the span both providers cover, and Anthropic resolves no fall in error. Instant maps also change with the notation. With reasoning allowed most do not, and release date adds no resolved gain in accuracy. Resolved means the 95% bootstrap interval on the yearly change excludes zero.

What the maps show

Scores are in kelvin and megapascals at 64 × 64 unless a sentence says otherwise.

Tidy is not the same as right

Instant maps are tidy: 84–98% of points sit in the largest connected region of their phase, against 100% for the truth. Their balanced accuracy still runs only from 0.63 to 0.83. Physical order means more pressure moves water to a denser phase and more heat to a lighter one, ranking solid, liquid, supercritical, vapor. Ice Ih breaks this rule and jitter adds false steps, so even the truth falls short of 100%. Where neighboring answers differ, the order is physical 54–69% of the time, against 94% for the truth on the same jittered points (99% at 128 × 128) and about 50% for a random map. Inside a region a stray answer adds two physical and two unphysical steps, which pulls the figure toward 50%: half the unphysical steps on the median map touch a cell none of its four neighbors matches. Six of the eight regions are mostly right, at 9–27% wrong on average. The misses gather in the liquid above the critical pressure (68%) and the vapor below 0 °C (56%), and on five maps most of the ordinary liquid, below the critical pressure, is missing.

Four facts beat every instant map on balanced accuracy

A sketch built from the triple point, the critical point, ice melting at 0 °C and one latent heat scores 0.94. No model answering instantly passes 0.83, and the best of the 132 maps across all six notations at 32 × 32 is Opus 4.7 in kelvin and pascals, at 0.90. The sketch is not better everywhere. It has no ice above 0 °C, so 21 of the 22 maps beat it on high-pressure ice, GPT-4o and GPT-6 Luna beat its melting-curve error, and it uses the textbook definition of supercritical, above both critical values, which the prompt does not state. Its layout is built in rather than derived from those facts, and it was chosen after the pilot. Of the 18 models tested both ways, 11 of the 17 with a reading boil water within 15 K of the truth at 1 atm instantly, and all 18 do with reasoning allowed.

The boiling curve closed in at OpenAI, the top of the melting curve overshot

At OpenAI, instant boiling-curve error fell from about 2 decades of pressure on GPT-4o mini, whose line there mostly marks where vapor turns to supercritical, to about 0.2 on GPT-5.5 and GPT-6 Sol. That fall is resolved in all six notations over the whole series, but not over the span both providers cover (a fall of 0.47 decades a year, −0.27 to 0.87). At Anthropic no notation resolves a trend in the error: Sonnet 4.5's 2 decades appears only in kelvin and megapascals, 0.6 to 1.2 elsewhere, Sonnet 4.6 under five months later reads 1.6 to 4.7, and while Sonnet 5.5 reaches 0.2, Haiku 5.5 is at 1.2. Its boiling line did rise, though, 1.55 decades a year (0.43 to 2.94), from far too low on Sonnet 4.5 to the truth on Sonnet 5.5. The two providers' falls in error cannot be told apart (0.49 and 0.75 decades a year; difference 0.26, −0.9 to 1.5). Melting-curve error did not fall: it runs from 27 to 107 K, and the worst values are on recent models, which put high-pressure ice far too hot. Thirteen of the 22, 12 of them from 2026, turn hot compressed liquid into ice below 1 GPa, where the true melting pressure is 3 to 12 GPa. With reasoning allowed, 10 of the 12 of these that have a reasoned arm put that line above 1 GPa.

Allowing reasoning lifted all 18 models tested both ways

Balanced accuracy rose on all 18, by a median of 0.20 and at least 0.06. Over the year both providers cover, neither instant trend is resolved in kelvin and megapascals: OpenAI −0.03 to 0.25 a year, Anthropic −0.21 to 0.11. With reasoning allowed, the melting curve improves on all 18, from a median of 77 K to 20 K. GPT-5.6 Sol, and GPT-6 Astra and GPT-6.1 Sol, which cannot turn reasoning off, match IAPWS at all but one or two of 39 pressures, on a grid with cells 11 K wide, including 8 or 9 of the 10 above 209 MPa, where the curve climbs. Reasoning also cuts the median boiling error from 0.58 to 0.006 decades and leaves 23 of 24 maps at or below the best instant map, 0.18.

Instant answers depend on the notation

At 32 × 32, all 22 instant models change their answers with the units more than they do on a plain repeat. Sonnet 4.6 agrees with itself on 96% of repeats and 76% of unit changes. Opus 5 calls hot gas below the critical pressure supercritical 85–89% of the time in kelvin and 18–22% in Celsius. With reasoning allowed, at 16 × 16, the gap between repeats and unit changes is 0.06 at most, and for 18 of 24 models it cannot be told from zero.

Reasoning concentrates near the lines between labels

Eight of the nine models that often skip reasoning reason more often near the boundaries and critical lines, and all nine spend more tokens there: Opus 5.5 reasons on 79% of nearby points and 20% of the rest, Sonnet 5.5 on 76% and 17%. Sonnet 5 reasons on fewer points near a line, 80% against 88% far from one, but spends more tokens there, 151 a reply against 93. Drawn on the map, the reasoning of Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 traces the sublimation and boiling curves and the critical pressure, then fills the map from about 200 MPa on Opus 5.5, apart from its coldest ice, and from about 1 GPa on Sonnet 5.5, even in the hot corner where no boundary lies. Where it can be tested, this tracks the model's own instant errors on three of four models: Sonnet 5.5 reasons on 82% of the points it gets wrong instantly and 28% of those it gets right, Opus 4.8 on 79% and 49%, Opus 5 on 75% and 65%. Much of that is location: those gaps of 53, 31 and 11 points shrink to 5, 9 and 7 within 16 × 16 cells. Sonnet 5 shows no such link overall, at 85% and 85%, though within cells it reasons 4 points more on its errors, and Opus 5.5 has no instant arm to test.

What allowing reasoning changes

Each row is one model. A hollow circle is the model answering with no reasoning, a filled circle is the same model with reasoning allowed, and the vertical line is the four-fact sketch. Haiku 5.5 gains the most as scored here, from 0.63 to 0.96: instantly it almost never says liquid. Accept supercritical on fluid and GPT-5.5 gains the most, 0.31 against Haiku 5.5's 0.19. Sonnet 5 gains the least, from 0.79 to 0.85: with reasoning allowed it calls 59% of hot gas supercritical, up from 24% instantly, the only one of the 18 whose vapor score falls. Accept that word on hot gas under both conditions and it goes from 0.82 to 0.91, still the second-smallest gain.

Fourteen of 24 models beat the sketch with reasoning allowed, nine of them among the 18 tested both ways: seven of eight at OpenAI and two of ten at Anthropic, though no setting is shared between the two providers, so this split does not compare them like for like. The near misses of GPT-5.1 and Sonnet 4.5 are within sampling error, and so is GPT-6 Luna's lead on these 4,096 points, though on its 128 × 128 map Luna leads the sketch by 0.012 (0.006 to 0.018).

Allowed does not mean used. Settings differ by model and some rarely reason: Fable 5.1 does so on 11% of replies. GPT-6 Astra averages 26 reasoning tokens a reply and scores 0.99. The table at the bottom gives every model's share.

Do the boundaries move toward the truth?

Partly. Answering instantly, a resolved fall in error appears only on OpenAI's boiling curve, over its whole series. With reasoning allowed, only Anthropic's melting curve shows one, and only just. Each line marks where one model stops saying vapor (boiling) or solid (melting) at 64 × 64, whatever it says next, so a model with little liquid still gets a line. Lines are a running median over five grid lines, drawn only where the answer changes, and colored by release date as in the key. The heavy lines are IAPWS.

Instantly, boiling curves spread over orders of magnitude of pressure and newer models gather around the truth. The melting lines gather in the wrong place: 13 of the 14 lines from 2026 sit at 0.7 GPa or below at 400 K, where the truth is 2.8 GPa, and 10 stay at 2 GPa or below at 600 K, where it is 9 GPa, further below it than older ones. The 14th, Haiku 5.5, goes the other way and stops at 342 K. The melting error comes from the top of the map. Over the 29 isobars below 209 MPa it runs from 8 to 43 K, with no resolved trend in its size. Over the 10 above it runs from 58 K to at least 382 K: most recent models put ice far too hot, and GPT-4o mini, GPT-4o, GPT-4.1 and both Haikus too cold. On four of those isobars Opus 5 says ice all the way to 900 K, so its offset stops at the map edge. Across notations at 32 × 32 all but GPT-4o stay too cold in all six, while GPT-4o turns too hot in atmospheres.

The count of models that turn hot compressed liquid into ice below 1 GPa moves with the notation: 13 of 22 in kelvin and megapascals at 64 × 64, and from 9 to 14 across notations at 32 × 32, where only GPT-6 Sol, Opus 4.8, Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 stay below in all six.

With reasoning allowed, the boiling curves land on the truth and most melting lines follow it up. Of the 12 of those 13 models with a reasoned arm, six reach about 6 GPa, read as the one pressure that best separates solid from the rest between 400 and 640 K, which is what the true labels give on these points. Across all 24 reasoned maps the ten melting lines closest to the truth are from 2026, as are 18 of the 24, though some 2026 models, such as Sonnet 5, still sit far below.

Across notations at 32 × 32 the slope runs from 0.04 to 0.11 and the verdict moves too: in all three Celsius notations no single omission brings zero back and the trend clears zero even over the shared span (0.03 to 0.19, 0.03 to 0.17, 0.04 to 0.28), while in kelvin and pascals zero is inside even over the whole series (−0.02 to 0.08). With reasoning allowed neither provider shows a resolved gain in accuracy: OpenAI 0.01 a year (−0.02 to 0.06), Anthropic 0.02 (−0.05 to 0.11).

Signed, Anthropic's line rose 1.55 decades a year (0.43 to 2.94), from far too low on Sonnet 4.5 to the truth on Sonnet 5.5, while OpenAI's shows no such move (−0.62 to 0.89). Answering instantly, small models lag on the boiling curve, ranking 15th to 21st of 22, and all but GPT-6 Luna on balanced accuracy too. Luna's error is 0.82 decades, against about 0.2 on GPT-6 Sol and Sonnet 5.5, yet it has the best instant balanced accuracy of any model, 0.83, and the second-lowest melting-curve error, 27 K. Where the picture breaks

Darker cells are points where more models answered wrong, at 64 × 64. Instantly, errors pile up at the liquid above the critical pressure, which models call supercritical or ice, at the vapor below 0 °C, along the top of the boiling curve, and at the hot gas just below the critical pressure, where 92% of the wrong answers are the word supercritical. With reasoning allowed, most of this clears: on the 18 models tested both ways, the share wrong on the liquid above the critical pressure, the vapor below 0 °C and the vapor within a decade below the critical pressure falls from 61% to 14%. What remains runs along the top of the melting curve and past the point near 705 K where it leaves the map, into the hot high-pressure corner, sits in the cold low-pressure corner, and lies as a pale wash over hot vapor, where Sonnet 5, Opus 5, Sonnet 4.6 and Haiku 4.5 say supercritical. Across all 24 models that wash holds 36% of the wrong answers left, and across the 18 tested both ways it holds 40%.

The same errors by region: the share of each region's points a model got wrong, darker for more. "Vapor below 0 °C" is the vapor side of the sublimation curve. Instantly, in kelvin and megapascals, Sonnet 4.5, GPT-4o mini, GPT-4.1, Haiku 4.5 and Opus 4.7 miss 92–100% of those points, drawing a wall of ice down to near vacuum: near 0 °C on Sonnet 4.5 and Opus 4.7, about 15 °C warmer on the others. In the same notation, from GPT-5.2 on, most OpenAI models swing the other way: they get those points right by putting the sublimation curve 0.8 to 3.6 decades too high, and lose ordinary ice instead, missing 22–54% of it. On GPT-5.2, 5.4 and 5.5 most of that ice is called vapor. On GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6.1 Terra a third to a half is called liquid, which the sublimation curve does not explain.

"Liquid above the critical pressure" is the largest miss on average: 68% instantly, 13% with reasoning allowed, or 65% and 15% on the 18 models tested both ways. Supercritical answers make up 39 of those 68 percentage points and 1 of the 13. The fluid changes smoothly across the critical temperature and pressure lines, so beside those lines the word is a naming miss rather than a physics miss, but only 13 of the 39 lie within 65 K of the critical temperature. The other 26 lie further below it, 6 of them below 400 K, where water is an ordinary dense liquid. Nearly all of "Vapor above the critical temperature" is the same word, more than half of it over a decade below the critical pressure. Counting every supercritical answer in those two rows as right, the liquid row falls to 29% and 11%. "Vapor below 0 °C" is then the largest miss instantly, at 56%, and roughly level with the liquid row with reasoning allowed, at 12% against 11%.

The same conditions in different units

Every point of the 32 × 32 grid was asked in six notations (16 × 16 with reasoning allowed): kelvin or Celsius, with pascals, megapascals or atmospheres. Two notations were also asked twice, which sets the noise floor, so a model only has a notation problem if its dots sit apart. Instantly, all 22 do, by up to 0.20. Noise can hide a gap: GPT-4o has the lowest agreement across units of any model, 75%, but it agrees with itself on only 81% of repeats, so its gap is only 0.06.

No unit is bad for everyone. Celsius versus kelvin makes no overall difference to instant balanced accuracy (0.01, interval −0.01 to 0.03), and atmospheres lift balanced accuracy only on everyday water (250–400 K, 10 kPa to 10 MPa, 57 points at 32 × 32), by 0.05 over pascals (0.01 to 0.08). Most of the damage is specific to each model, though Celsius shifts cold answers from ice toward vapor across models, cutting misses of the vapor below 0 °C by 0.12 (0.04 to 0.20) and adding 0.06 on ordinary ice (0.01 to 0.11). Sonnet 4.5 misses all of the vapor below 0 °C in kelvin and megapascals and almost none of it in atmospheres. Sonnet 4.6 misses 73–100% of it in every notation except Celsius and megapascals, where it misses 4%. Part of a gap can be one word: forgive supercritical on fluid, and Opus 5's gap falls from 0.16 to 0.05.

A change of unit can also change the number's form, so a gap is not purely a unit effect, and no arm rewords a prompt without changing its units. At 64 × 64, at the switch to 1.000e6 pascals, where the true answer does not change, vapor answers on Haiku 5.5, GPT-5.2 and GPT-6 Luna fall 24 to 48 percentage points more than the average at the same pressures in megapascals and atmospheres, and clearly more than in either alone. GPT-5.1 falls 49 points, but its megapascals fall 38 at the same place, with no scientific notation involved. Only those three show a step that neither control shares, against about 1 of the 16 at cuts where pascals do not change form. The contrast with the average alone clears zero on 7 at the switch and about 4 at those cuts, so a count of models clear of zero says little. Megapascals cross 1.000 at the same pressure, and within 0.15 decades of it GPT-4.1, GPT-5.1 and GPT-4o lose 30 to 41 points of vapor.

How this was done

Questions

Each question is one user message: "Pure water at equilibrium, at 450.00 kelvin and 2.000 megapascals: solid, liquid, vapor, or supercritical? Do not say anything else." Pressures are plain decimals from 0.001 up to 1,000,000 of the unit and scientific notation outside that range, such as 1.230e-8 megapascals. The request sets no system prompt, though Anthropic adds its own describing the schema. The grid holds one jittered point per cell, from 16 × 16 up to 128 × 128, over 170–900 K and 0.01 Pa to 20 GPa. A draw within 2 K or 0.03 decades of a boundary or critical line is redrawn in the same cell, so no cell is empty and no label turns on where a curve sits within its IAPWS uncertainty.

Truth

IAPWS R14-08 for the melting and sublimation curves, and the saturation equation of Wagner and Pruss from IAPWS SR1-86(1992), which agrees with IAPWS-95 to within 0.01%. Supercritical means above both the critical temperature and the critical pressure. Hot gas below the critical pressure counts as vapor, and cold fluid above it counts as liquid. Those two edges are naming conventions rather than phase boundaries, and the prompt does not state them.

No tools

The request code accepts a fixed list of keys, none of which can carry a tool, and raises on anything else. A schema enforced by each provider limits a reply to the four phase words. Of about 1.2 million replies, 23 escaped it: two bare phase words count as that word, and the other 21 count as wrong. Fallbacks are off.

Two conditions

Instant means no reasoning. Reasoning allowed means a setting that let the model reason in tests, low on all but one model: a 1,024-token budget on the five oldest Claude models, adaptive thinking at low effort on Opus 4.8, Opus 5, Sonnet 5.5 and Haiku 5.5, medium effort on Sonnet 5, where low effort skipped thinking on most calls, and low effort on eight OpenAI models. Those tests are not stored here. The six models that cannot turn reasoning off run at their lowest effort. GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini and GPT-4.1 have no reasoning. Opus 4.7 never engaged adaptive thinking in 15 hand-run calls, which are not stored here, and it rejects a fixed budget.

Scoring

Balanced accuracy averages the four phases, because 61% of the grid counts as vapor here, 26 percentage points of it hot gas above the critical temperature. Boundary error compares a model's boundary with the one the true labels give on the same points, so a perfect map scores 0. Sublimation is scored by region, because many maps have no such boundary to measure. Trends in kelvin and megapascals use the 64 × 64 grid, the finest that every model has. Trends across notations use 32 × 32.

These scores use the textbook definition of supercritical and call hot gas vapor. Accepting supercritical for hot gas below the critical pressure moves the best instant score from 0.83 to 0.84 and the median gain from reasoning from 0.20 to 0.19. Also accepting it for fluid above the critical pressure from 0.9 of the critical temperature moves them to 0.87 and 0.17, and at any temperature to 0.92 and 0.14. The sketch stays at 0.94 because it uses this naming, and it still beats every instant map.

About half the weight of balanced accuracy sits on points whose class is set by the critical temperature and pressure lines, and OpenAI's instant trend depends on them: 0.05 a year (0.01 to 0.10) as scored, 0.04 (−0.001 to 0.10) accepting supercritical on hot gas, 0.03 (−0.02 to 0.10) also from 0.9 of the critical temperature, and −0.02 (−0.09 to 0.06) at any temperature. That last tolerance also credits a map for calling water at 300 K supercritical, so it hides ordinary liquid the map is missing. The five OpenAI models that largely stop saying supercritical on compressed liquid, GPT-5.4, GPT-5.5, GPT-5.6 Sol, GPT-6 Sol and GPT-6.1 Terra (0–11% of it, against 84–94% on the three oldest), do say liquid more, 51–69%, but they now say ice on 30–45% of it, against 3–8% on the three oldest.

Cost

Limits

Retirements cut Anthropic's history off at September 2025 and OpenAI's at July 2024, so the two lines cover different spans. Release date stands in for generation. Reasoning settings differ by model, and newer models reason less at both providers, so a reasoned trend does not isolate generation. Default sampling applies throughout, and each prompt is asked once or twice. Intervals are not adjusted for how many were examined: 26 of 143 trends and 13 of 84 notation contrasts exclude zero. They resample models, and consecutive releases are related. Ice VII above the critical temperature is barely sampled. Six GPT-6.1 Terra answers survive without their raw replies, after an id collision that is now fixed. The harness, the analysis and this page were written with Claude Code running Claude Opus 5.5, which is one of the graded models.
